Report-only evidence on whether a Hurst-based entry gate is worth building. Nothing here is wired to the scheduler, to config, or to any live path. It supersedes the #1410 study, whose own render lives at `hurst_1410_gate_calibration.md`.

#1410 returned INCONCLUSIVE on a single re-used sample, a 30-hypothesis grid, and a null that treated correlated concurrent trades as independent. This study fixes all three and MEASURES the detection limit. That last part is what makes a null result readable: with a measured limit beside the observed separation, an inconclusive verdict says WHICH of two very different things happened — the edge is too small to matter, or the edge is visible and the tested RULE fails to capture it. The Recommendation section states which one this run found, derived from the numbers rather than assumed.

## Pre-registered design

These constants live at the top of the study script and were fixed before the sweep ran. The Recommendation is the mechanical output of the acceptance rule applied to them.

- Estimator: `hurst_exponent` from `shared_strategies/open/indicators_core.py` (#1409 SSoT). Never reimplemented here.
- NaN policy: NaN is its OWN bucket for BOTH H and ADX, never coerced to 0.5 / 25. It neither arms nor disarms the gate (state holds) and gives a size multiplier of exactly 1.
- Hurst window lengths: 128, 256, 512 bars.
- Buckets on H at entry: `<0.45`, `0.45-0.50`, `0.50-0.55`, `>=0.55`, `NaN`.
- History floor: 2020-01-01. Data source exchange: `binanceus`. Fee model: `hyperliquid` (plus the Backtester's 5 bps slippage default). The two platform axes are independent and are never coupled.
- Datasets (21): `BTC/USDT 1h`, `BTC/USDT 4h`, `ETH/USDT 1h`, `ETH/USDT 4h`, `SOL/USDT 1h`, `SOL/USDT 4h`, `BTC/USDT 2h`, `ETH/USDT 2h`, `SOL/USDT 2h`, `BNB/USDT 1h`, `BNB/USDT 4h`, `XRP/USDT 1h`, `XRP/USDT 4h`, `DOGE/USDT 1h`, `DOGE/USDT 4h`, `LTC/USDT 1h`, `LTC/USDT 4h`, `ADA/USDT 1h`, `ADA/USDT 4h`, `LINK/USDT 1h`, `LINK/USDT 4h`.
- Windows: `2020H2` 2020-07-01..2021-01-01; `2021` 2021-01-01..2022-01-01; `2022` 2022-01-01..2023-01-01; `2023` 2023-01-01..2024-01-01; `2024` 2024-01-01..2025-01-01; `2025H1` 2025-01-01..2025-07-01; `is` 2025-06-10..2026-01-01; `oos` 2026-01-01..latest.
- Primary cohort economics: protocol `2021`, `2022`; held-out `2020H2`, `is`, `oos`, `2023`, `2024`, `2025H1`.
- Exploratory cohort economics: #1410's split verbatim — protocol `is`, `oos`; held-out `2023`, `2024`, `2025H1`.
- Volume floors on EFFECTIVE N: >= 20 suppressed and >= 30 kept. Held-out rule: drawdown must not degrade on >= 2/3 of the held-out windows carrying legs, with at least 3 such windows.
- Inference: free-shuffle permutation (the #1410 null, for continuity) AND a shared circular calendar rotation (the cluster null, which the verdict reads). 10000 draws, seed 1422, minimum offset 30 days. Benjamini-Hochberg at alpha=0.05, applied SEPARATELY to the primary and exploratory cohorts.
- Rotation on ragged spans: offsets are drawn against the pool's SHORTEST span, so every retained dataset hosts every offset and two concurrent datasets always shift by the same calendar amount. Drawing against the longest span instead would push offsets past a shorter dataset's end, and both ways out of that cost something: leaving it unrotated hands the null its observed alignment (p too high), and wrapping it rotates it by a different calendar amount than its neighbours, which understates the correlation the null exists to preserve (p too LOW). The false positive is the worse failure, so the range is capped and the wrap survives only as a dormant guard. Narrowest shared range in this run: 646 distinct offsets. A dataset under 90 days cannot host the `[30, span-30]` band at all and leaves the contrast, the counts and the effective-N floors together, never just the offset range.
- Minimum detectable effect: deterministic shift of the suppressed group — each family's OWN anti-signal side, so the injection runs in the direction that family's gate would exploit — grid 0.1 pp to 5 pp then a 0.02 pp refinement, scored against the rank-1 Benjamini-Hochberg threshold with 2000 draws.
- Joint table: ADX period 14 (Wilder), split at 25 (the composite classifier default). Its significance p and contrast-local detection limit both use the inference resolution of 10000 draws and seed 1422; they cannot disagree at the boundary merely because one used the lower 2000-draw general MDE resolution.

### Cohorts — why the primary set is trustworthy

The four PRIMARY hypotheses were chosen by reading #1410's raw p-values (per family and mode, the smallest). That selection is only legitimate because the primary cohort is DISJOINT from the data those p-values came from: a cell is primary iff it is absent from #1410's 6-dataset x 5-window grid — a pre-2023 window, or a symbol #1410 never scored. ### Look-ahead invariant

Rolling H at bar `i` uses closes `[i-W+1, i]`. The DECISION series is that shifted one bar, so a signal at bar N reads H through N-1; the backtester fills a bar-N signal at N+1's open, so the FILL-BAR stamp is the bar-close series shifted twice. Entry ADX uses the SAME two shifts, so both features in the joint table sit on one look-ahead rule. ADX warm-up bars are masked to NaN — `compute_regime` fills them with 0.0, which would otherwise file every warm-up bar under "low ADX".

## Coverage and effective sample size

153 of 168 (dataset, window) cells carried enough history to score: 514 bars of lead before the window start, and at least 80% of the bars a complete cache would hold inside the window. That density floor is what catches a delisting gap — a year present as a few hundred bars is not a year. Effective N is `N^2 / sum_ij rho_ij`, with `rho_ij` the symbol-level daily-return correlation when two trades' holding periods OVERLAP and 0 when they do not; same-symbol pairs are the same tape and take 1.0, and correlations are clipped to `[0, 1]` so anti-correlation can never manufacture power. ## Measured detection limit

The smallest per-trade edge (percentage points of net return) each design could have detected, under the cluster null at the rank-1 Benjamini-Hochberg threshold. This is the number that turns "inconclusive" from a mystery into a bound.

| Pool | Cluster MDE (pp/trade) | Free MDE (pp/trade) | Largest separation ON THAT POOL (pp/trade) | Resolvable? | Cluster p at zero injection | Free p at zero injection |
|------|-----------------------:|--------------------:|------------------------------------------:|:-----------:|----------------------------:|-------------------------:|
| #1410 design (its 30-hypothesis grid) | 0.38 | 0.46 | 1.04 | yes | 0.4583 | 0.3958 |
| this study, primary cohort | 0.96 | 0.82 | 0.37 | NO | 1.0000 | 0.9990 |
| this study, exploratory grid | 0.42 | 0.38 | 0.99 | yes | 0.4923 | 0.4138 |

The "p at zero injection" columns are the SAME contrast with no injected edge: each family's suppressed side against its kept side — `H < 0.5` for a family that arms on high H, `H >= 0.5` for one that arms on low H. The separation column is that same contrast measured on the SAME rows as that pool's limit, so the two are comparable; a whole-study separation read against a sub-cohort's limit would not be. `Resolvable? = NO` means the separation sits under the limit and the design cannot see an effect that small — such a pool's null bounds the edge from above and says nothing about whether a smaller one exists.

## Part A - outcomes bucketed by H at entry

Ungated legs only, pooled per family across datasets and windows and deduplicated on `(strategy, symbol, timeframe, entry_date)`. Drawdown here is TRADE-GRANULAR (the compounded trade sequence), not the bar-level engine drawdown used in Part B.

The `NaN` bucket is EMPTY here because of the harness, not the estimator, and this run MEASURED the condition that makes it so. H at a scored bar needs 514 bars of history before the earliest window start; the thinnest dataset in this run carried 627, so H is defined on every scored bar. On a thinner cache the run prints a warm-up warning and this bucket carries real trades. A live consumer has a bounded fetch depth and WILL see NaN at start-up and after a data gap, so the NaN policy still has to be decided: NaN is unknown, never 0.5, and it holds the gate state.

## Acceptance rule

A primary config wins for its family only when ALL of the following hold:

1. Effective suppressed trades >= 20 and effective kept trades >= 30.
2. Significant after Benjamini-Hochberg at alpha=0.05 ON THE CLUSTER p, within the primary cohort's own denominator.
3. On BOTH primary protocol windows: mean drawdown magnitude falls, chop loss falls, and the return give-up stays within max(1 pp, 0.1 x |ungated return|).
4. Drawdown does not degrade on at least 2/3 of the held-out windows that carry legs, with at least 3 such windows.

Rules 1 and 2 are what separate a real edge from arithmetic. ANY gate that removes trades lowers drawdown and chop loss on a losing book, so those columns alone prove nothing — that is exactly what produced #1410's 16 economics-only configs. The cluster permutation asks the question a gate must answer, with correlated concurrent trades moving together under the null: are the trades this gate SUPPRESSES worse than the ones it KEEPS, beyond what a calendar rotation of the same labels would produce? And effective N stops a pool of correlated rows from clearing a volume floor it has no independent information to clear.

## Recommendation

INCONCLUSIVE

No configuration of the 4 primary hypotheses passed the pre-registered acceptance rule. 0 reached Benjamini-Hochberg significance on the cluster permutation at alpha=0.05; 0 were untestable. The strongest primary hypothesis reached cluster p=0.0485 (`momentum/gate/W512/arm0.52/dis0.48`). Measured detection limits under the cluster null: the #1410 design could resolve 0.38 pp per trade; this expanded design resolves 0.96 pp per trade. The largest raw anti-signal separation on that same cohort is 0.37 pp per trade, BELOW the 0.96 pp the design can resolve. A separation under the limit is INVISIBLE to this design, so the null result excludes an edge of 0.96 pp or larger and says nothing either way about one the size the buckets show. Power is the binding constraint here, not the absence of a signal.

Do not build a Hurst entry gate on this evidence. That is where this agrees with #1410; the reason differs, and the reason is what should drive what happens next.

This run did NOT resolve the separation its own buckets show, so its null is a statement about the design's power and not about the market. Do not read it as evidence that no edge exists. What the run does establish is an upper bound: an edge at or above the primary cohort's detection limit would have been caught, and none was. Settling the smaller effect needs a design that can see it — a sharper contrast, a larger primary cohort, or fewer pre-registered hypotheses sharing the correction — and it must re-register those hypotheses BEFORE looking at these numbers, or it inherits exactly the selection problem this design was built to avoid.

One reading the table supports and the pre-registered test cannot: pools outside the confirmatory cohort carry more rows, so they resolve a separation this design can see, and it does not survive the cluster null — the #1410 design's cells (1.04 pp separation against a 0.38 pp limit, cluster p=0.4583); this study's exploratory grid (0.99 pp separation against a 0.42 pp limit, cluster p=0.4923). That contrast is EXPLORATORY: it was not pre-registered, and on those rows the split is chosen after seeing them. It licenses no gate. What it does show is that the visible separation there is consistent with whole datasets moving together, rather than with H sorting trades within them.

#1411's `hurst_gate` stays DEFAULT-OFF with no recommended thresholds. Nothing in this report licenses shipping one.
